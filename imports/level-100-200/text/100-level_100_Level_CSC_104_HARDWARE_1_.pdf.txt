MICROPROCESSORS 
 Microprocessor is a computer processor that is found in most modern 
personal computers, smartphones, and other electronic devices. It is a central 
processing unit (CPU) that performs most of the processing tasks in a computer 
system. The microprocessor is a key component of a computer, as it controls the 
fetching, decoding, and execution of instructions that are stored in memory. You 
can say that microprocessor is used as the brain of the computing devices which 
control overall execution and operations. The development of microprocessors has 
played a significant role in the evolution of computers and has made it possible for 
them to become smaller, faster, and more powerful over time. It works extremely 
fast, processing billions of instructions per second. Modern microprocessors have 
multiple cores, allowing them to handle several tasks at once. 
    
 
RAM (RANDOM ACCESS MEMORY) 
 While the microprocessor does the thinking, RAM (Random Access 
Memory) holds the data it needs. Imagine RAM as a workspace where a student 
places books while studying. If the table is large (more RAM), the student can 
keep more books open at once, making learning faster. If the table is small (less 
RAM), they have to keep putting books away and bringing them back, slowing

TYPES OF PROCESSORS AND THEIR FUNCTIONS 
1. General-Purpose Processors (CPU) 
These are the most common processors found in computers and laptops. They 
handle a wide range of tasks, from browsing the internet to running complex 
software. Examples include: Intel Core series (i3, i5, i7, i9) – Used in personal 
computers for general tasks. 


--- page 4 ---
 
2. Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) 
A GPU is specialized for handling images, videos, and gaming. Unlike a CPU that 
handles a variety of tasks, a GPU focuses on rendering visuals. It’s like an artist 
who only works on painting rather than doing multiple jobs. Examples include: 
NVIDIA GeForce & AMD Radeon – Used in gaming and video editing.  
Integrated GPUs – Built into the CPU, handling basic graphics tasks. 


--- page 5 ---
 
3. Digital Signal Processor (DSP) 
A digital signal processor (DSP) is a specialized microprocessor designed to 
perform high-speed mathematical operations on digital signals, which are 
sequences of digital data representing physical quantities such as sound, images, 
and other sensory information. DSPs are used in a range of applications that 
require fast processing of digital signals, including audio and video processing, 
telecommunications, and scientific computing. 
Function: Handles real-time signal processing, such as audio, video, and radar 
signals. Where Used: Smartphones (for audio processing), Medical imaging 
(MRI, CT scans), Voice recognition systems (Siri, Google Assistant), etc

4. Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) 
Unlike ASICs (which are fixed), FPGAs can be reprogrammed to change how they 
function, making them useful for testing new technology. 
Function: A flexible processor that can be programmed for different applications. 
Where Used: Aerospace and defense systems, AI and robotics, 5G and 
telecommunications

5. Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) 
TPUs are much faster than CPUs and GPUs when it comes to training AI models. 
Function: Designed specifically for machine learning and artificial intelligence. 
Where Used: Google AI projects (e.g., Google Photos, Google Translate), Self-
driving cars, Deep learning models

6. Neuro-processing Unit (NPU) 
NPUs learn from patterns, improving performance in tasks like facial recognition 
and speech processing. 
Function: Mimics the human brain’s neural network for faster AI computations. 
Where Used: 
AI-powered smartphones (Huawei Kirin NPU, Apple Neural Engine), Advanced 
robotics, Smart assistants (Alexa, Google Assistant)

7. Application-Specific Integrated Circuit (ASIC) 
Unlike general-purpose CPUs, ASICs are optimized for one specific function, 
making them highly efficient for that task 
Function: Designed for a single task, like cryptocurrency mining or video 
encoding. Where Used: Bitcoin mining (ASIC miners), AI and machine learning 
(Google’s TPU), Networking hardware (routers, modems)

8. Microcontrollers 
Microcontrollers are small, low-power processors used in embedded systems like 
washing machines, traffic lights, and smart home devices. They don’t need high 
power because they perform simple, repetitive tasks. Popular examples: 
ARM Cortex series – Found in smartphones and smartwatches. 
Arduino & PIC microcontrollers – Used in robotics and automation. 
 
9. Server Processors 
These are heavy-duty processors designed for cloud computing and handling large 
amounts of data. They work like factory managers, ensuring multiple tasks are 
completed efficiently. Examples include: 
Intel Xeon & AMD EPYC – Used in data centers and high-performance 
computing.
