# Philosophical Arguments in Support of the Existence of God
Cosmological argument:
It was St. Thomas Aquinas’ adaptation of Aristole’s philosophy of the “Prime Mover.”
It is therefore also called the argument of “cause.”
It is used by Christian theologians, appealing to logic, to show that God created the world and therefore must exist.
“Every action is set in motion; nothing happens by chance.”


--- slide 21 ---
# Teleological argument
It is an argument that all things in  nature necessarily depend on other things in order for them to exist.
Major philosophers who taught this: Immanuel Kant, Thomas Aquinas.
There is order, pattern or design in the universe which proves the existence of an Intelligence behind this design: design-designer paradigm.
Design is seen in every sphere of nature; macro or micro levels, plant or animals, terrestrial or celestial.
Teleo means distant/end/goal while logos means study/speech/word. It is the understanding that the world is a design, something preplanned.
Also called the Intelligent design argument.
This world is a design and the designer is God.


--- slide 22 ---
# Ontological Argument
Is a branch of metaphysics dealing with state of being or existence.
Early church father, St. Anslem gave theological meaning to Plato’s philosophy on ontology.
It does not try to prove the existence of God, but assumes it and measure virtue relative to God who is recognized as the Supreme Reality.
The fact that people strive to be better people, more virtuous means there is a perfect being who people strive to be like i.e. God.


--- slide 23 ---
# The Religious Argument
Rests on the fact that it seems humans all over the world in all cultures often exhibit religious traits/tendencies (regardless of their varying theological interpretations or supreme being) is the evidence there must be God.
St. Augustine was a leader in this school of thought.
God must have put the desire for religion in people’s heart so that we can seek God and know God better.


--- slide 24 ---
# Epistemological Argument
Epistemology is the study of how we know, acquire truth/information.
This argument is based on prowess of humans in acquiring knowledge in various fields. It wonders if it is possible for beings like humans to have existed by chance. It claims God, who is the Ultimate Intelligence, must have created humans in that manner

# Definition of Some Concepts and Paradigms of God
Monotheism: Is the belief in and the worship of only one God. It denies the possible existence of any other god. E.g Islam, Judaism, Christianity, (ATR?).
Polytheism: Is the belief in and the worship of more than one god. E.g. ancient religions of Egypt, Rome, Greece, (ATR?).
Pantheon: Is a house of worship (temple) where many gods are placed and worshipped. 


--- slide 27 ---
# Definition of concepts ctd.
Henotheism: Is the belief and worship of one god while accepting the possible existence of other gods. Henotheists will be loyal to their one god, but they acknowledge the existence of other (ethnic, tribal) gods.
Pantheism: Is the belief that God is in nature and nature is God. They are one and the same. Everything in nature has God in it.
Deism: Is the belief that God created the world, but no longer involved in the world; natural laws govern the world and there are no divine interventions in course of nature.


--- slide 28 ---
# Definition of concepts ctd.
Animism: is the belief that spirits fill creation and inhabit them such that all nature are not really inanimate. Mountains, trees, rivers etc all have spirits inhabiting them.
Dynamism: is the belief that certain, not all, nature have spirits or special force called “manna” in them. So certain tree or a particular river may have manna which qualified it to be revered or worshipped. 
New Age: Very complex and difficult to define. But it is a mix of certain religious beliefs such as in meditation, life in planes, communication with spirits etc.


--- slide 29 ---
# Definitions ctd.
Monism: here no distinction between certain spheres of existence; matter and God are not different. Matter and mind are one and the same.
Gnosticism: it literarily means “knowledge” or “to know.” It is multi-layers of beliefs; it is a pre-Christian philosophy, religion or world view. It teaches that the material world is a creation of the demiurge, and it is inferior, rather we should pursue the higher world of the spirit. To them, liberation is in sacrificing personal comfort even to the point of poverty, sexual abstinence etc.


--- slide 30 ---
# Definitions ctd.
Agnosticism: It believes the possibility of the existence of God, but insists that God is too big to be known. Humans cannot know God, there is therefore no reason to talk about God because we do not know.
Theism: The general description of the belief in the existence of God or gods and with varying level of involvement with the world,
Atheism: The belief that there is no God/gods. There is no supernatural powers involved in nature/creation. God is the creation of human mind. Etc.

# Open/Process theism
In thinking about the difficulties arising from our understanding about the nature of God, Open/Process Theism is developed.

Process Philosophy tries to explain how we understand, comprehend and interact with our world; It is a form of epistemology (study of how we know or acquire truth/knowledge).  


--- slide 105 ---
# Process or Open Theology
Process philosophy says that the state of being is dynamic; no thing or person is ever the same. Therefore the whole world and state of existence keep changing in new and dynamic ways.
Process or Open theology: therefore objects to the traditional views about how God knows and relates to the world. 
Some Open theists include: Richard Rice, Clark Pinnock, Gregory Boyd and David Bassinger among others.


--- slide 106 ---
# Open Theism Objections
God’s Foreknowledge: We traditionally know God as knowing the future; Open theists say it is impossible to know the future because the future does not exist until it happens, therefore it is impossible to know what is not in existence. 
Because the material world and the state of existence is always changing, God cannot not know the future.
The text usually referred to about God knowing the future is misinterpretations of the texts.

# Open Theism

faithfulness to bible demands belief in God’s foreknowledge.
Prophets’ assignments indicate God’s foreknowledge.

 Also the bible records that God “repented.”
