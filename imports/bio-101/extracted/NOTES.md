Others: anatomy, biochemistry, cell biology, ecology, genetics, microbiology, astrobiology, bioengineering, bioinformatics, conservation biology, developmental biology, epidemiology, evolutionary biology, immunology, marine biology, paleontology, physiology.

## Chapter 2 — Living Things (book 7–12, pdf 009–014)

- Things = living or non-living. Living: breathe, eat, grow, move, reproduce, sense; made of cells; metabolism (anabolism + catabolism); finite lifespan.
- Plants vs animals: both eukaryotic. Animals motile, heterotrophic, no cell wall. Plants non-motile, photosynthetic, cell wall. Bacteria/archaea: prokaryotes, unicellular, cell walls of different materials, no nucleus.
- Cell: protoplasm + plasma membrane. Plant extras: large central vacuole, chloroplast, cell wall.
- Energy: photosynthesis (light → chemical) and cellular respiration (organic fuel → ATP). Anabolism builds complex molecules using energy; catabolism breaks them and releases energy. Metabolism = both. Nutrition, respiration, excretion are metabolic.
- Autotrophs make food (green plants, some bacteria). Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2 (light, chlorophyll). Light (Hill) reaction: chlorophyll absorbs light, splits water, NADP reducing power + energy. Dark phase: CO2 fixation. Chemosynthesis: colourless bacteria use chemical energy (N/S/Fe), not light. Macronutrients N P S K Mg Ca Fe; micronutrients B Mn Zn Cu Mo.
- Heterotrophs = consumers (herbivore / carnivore / omnivore).
- Respiration: energy from food. External = gas to respiratory surface; internal = ATP (glycolysis, Krebs, ETC), aerobic or anaerobic. Organs: lungs (mammals), gills (fish), body surface (amoeba). Aerobic: glucose + O2 → CO2 + H2O + ATP. Anaerobic: glucose → ethanol + CO2 + energy.
- Excretion: remove toxic metabolic wastes. Animals: skin, lungs, liver, kidneys (sweat, urea, ammonia, uric acid). Plants: O2, CO2, transpiration, guttation; store tannin/resin.
- Reproduction: sexual (gametes → zygote) vs asexual (binary fission, budding, vegetative). Growth: more cells or larger cells (stem cells / meristems). Response: receptors; human senses plus vestibular and thermoreception. Movement: animals locomote; plants nastic movement. Death: senescence.
- Living vs non-living table: life, reproduce, need food/air/water, stimuli, metabolism, growth, lifespan, self-movement, respiration. Examples: humans/plants vs rock/pen/gadgets.

## Chapter 3 — Classification (book 13–19, pdf 015–021)

- Classification = grouping by similarities/differences. >1.8 million described species. Taxonomy/systematics. Aristotle first attempt; Linnaeus present system (physical characters; genetics later).
- Hierarchy: Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Sub-/super- prefixes. Species = can mate and produce fertile offspring. Binomial: Genus + species.
- Five kingdoms (as presented): Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Placement uses food, cell type, cell number.
- **Monera:** prokaryotic, non-cellulose cell wall, no nuclear membrane, unicellular, auto or hetero. Bacteria, cyanobacteria (*Nostoc*, *Spirulina*). Bacteria: capsule, wall (except *Mycoplasma*), membrane, pili, flagella, nucleoid, ribosomes; saprotroph/parasite/symbiont. Cyanobacteria: photosynthetic prokaryotes; no mitochondria/Golgi/chloroplasts/ER; gas vesicles; uni- or filamentous.
- **Protista:** from *protistos* “the very first”; eukaryotic, usually unicellular, nucleus + organelles, often wall, flagella/cilia/pseudopodia. Aquatic/moist; some multicellular (giant kelp). Auto or hetero. Symbiosis (kelp–otters–urchins). Parasites (*Trypanosoma* sleeping sickness). Asexual common; sexual rare (stress).
- **Fungi:** yeasts, moulds, mushrooms. Eukaryotic, heterotrophic, non-motile, non-vascular, spores, no chlorophyll, chitin, sexual/asexual, pheromones, some parasitic. (Source also says they store food as starch.)
- **Plantae:** eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic, chlorophyll in plastids, cell wall, large vacuole, non-motile, vegetative or sexual reproduction.
- **Animalia:** largest kingdom; multicellular eukaryotes, no chlorophyll/cell wall, heterotrophic. Phyla listed: Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata.

## Chapter 4 — Cell types and cell theory (book 20–30, pdf 022–032)

- Cell = structural and functional unit of life; 1–100 μm. Unicellular: *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Chlamydomonas*. Multicellular: human, cat, *Spirogyra*. Functions: nutrients, wastes, stimuli, reproduction.
- Hooke 1665 cork “cells” (*Micrographia*). Leeuwenhoek animalcules, sperm 1677. Schleiden (plants) & Schwann (animals). Virchow 1855: all cells from cells. Sutton & Boveri: chromosomes carry heredity. Watson & Crick DNA. Thomson 1998 embryonic stem cells; iPS cells.
- Origin of cells: meteorites / deep-sea vents / lightning in reducing atmosphere. RNA likely first self-replicator. Cells ~4.0–4.3 bya, probably heterotrophs. Lipid bilayers form vesicles.
- Levels: chemical → cell → tissue (epithelial, muscle, nervous, connective) → organ (heart, liver, kidney) → system (digestive, nervous, endocrine, circulatory, respiratory, urinary, reproductive). Cellular vs organ physiology.
- Prokaryote = before nucleus; eukaryote = true nucleus. All cells: DNA, plasma membrane, cytoplasm/cytosol, ribosomes. Same genetic code. Differences: size, nucleus vs nucleoid, linear vs circular DNA, histones/chromosomes vs naked DNA + plasmids, ribosome complexity, membrane organelles. Table: prokaryote 0.1–5 μm, no nucleus, unicellular; eukaryote 5–100 μm, nucleus, uni or multi.
- Plant vs animal: plant larger/fixed/rectangular, cellulose wall, plastids/chloroplasts, large vacuole, nucleus aside, starch, few mitochondria, no centrioles/cilia typically. Animal irregular, plasma membrane only, glycogen, many mitochondria, centrioles, lysosomes, central nucleus.
- Cell theory (Schleiden 1838, Schwann 1839, Virchow 1855): (1) all living things made of cells (2) cell is smallest unit of a living thing (3) new cells from pre-existing cells.
- Review answers: cell; prokaryotic; Schleiden and Schwann; multicellular; prokaryotic and eukaryotic; cytoplasm/ribosomes; organ.

## Chapter 5 — Activities within the cell (book 31–44, pdf 033–046)

- **Osmosis:** water/solvent through partially permeable membrane from high water (low solute) to low water (high solute). Passive. Isotonic: no net movement. Hypotonic: more water in (turgid/endosmosis). Hypertonic: water out (flaccid/plasmolysis/exosmosis). Animal cells lyse in hypotonic, need isotonic. Plant cells prefer hypotonic (turgor); isotonic → droop. Osmotic pressure stops osmosis. Significance: nutrients/wastes, root water to xylem, turgor, transpiration balance. Examples: root water, guard cells, freshwater fish, cholera reverse flow.
- **Diffusion:** molecules high → low concentration until equal. Simple (through membrane, no protein) vs facilitated (carrier). Factors: temperature, concentration, distance, size, membrane. Dialysis = selected solutes across selectively permeable membrane. Examples: tea, perfume, sugar, incense, noodles. Significance: CO2 out, ions, soil water into root hairs, nerve ions. Osmosis vs diffusion table: osmosis needs membrane and water, one way, can reverse with pressure; diffusion any state, all directions, not stopped.
- **Cell division:** mitosis (identical body cells, 46→46) vs meiosis (gametes, 46→23). Cycle: G1, S (DNA + centrosome), G2, M (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase) + cytokinesis. Interphase = G1+S+G2.
- Mitosis phases: prophase (envelope breaks, spindle, sister chromatids); prometaphase (kinetochores); metaphase (metaphase plate); anaphase (sisters apart); telophase (envelope reforms); cytokinesis (cell plate in plants; cleavage in animals).
- Meiosis I: homologous pairing, synapsis, crossing over (chiasmata), bivalents at metaphase I, homologues separate. Meiosis II: sisters separate like mitosis. Diversity from crossing over + random assortment (~8 million combinations in humans).
- Significance: growth, repair, reproduction, binary fission in unicells, gametes, chromosome number, development. Mitosis vs meiosis table (identical sisters vs pairing/crossing over; 4 haploid vs 2 diploid daughters).

## Chapter 6 — Heredity (book 45–51, pdf 047–053)

- Heredity = passing traits via genes parent → offspring. Variation from DNA copying errors, gene–environment interaction, mutations; asexual variation smaller.
- Why it matters: diversity, genetics, medicine, evolution, biotech, conservation, ethics.
- Aristotle blending inheritance (wrong). Mendel (1822–1884) pea experiments: height, flower colour, seed texture. Discrete units (genes) in pairs (alleles).
- Law of segregation: alleles separate in gamete formation; offspring gets one from each parent.
- Independent assortment: different-trait alleles segregate independently.
- Dominance: dominant allele masks recessive in heterozygote. F2 ~ 3:1 phenotype.
- Monohybrid: one contrasting pair (tall × dwarf). F1 all tall (no medium). F2 3 tall : 1 short; genotype 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. One T enough for tall.
- Dihybrid: two pairs (round-yellow × wrinkled-green).
- Terms: gene (DNA unit, can mutate); alleles (alternative forms); chromosomes (DNA + protein threads, carry genes); genotype (allele set); phenotype (expressed traits); dominant capital / recessive lowercase; homozygous same alleles; heterozygous different.

## Chapter 7 — Genes and chromosomes (book 52–61, pdf 054–063)

- Genes = DNA segments (instructions). Chromosomes = thread-like DNA+protein in nucleus. Humans 23 pairs. DNA = two polynucleotide chains (double helix) of A, C, T, G. ~3 billion bases; ~99% same, 1% unique. DNA and RNA are nucleic acids (with carbs, proteins, lipids).
- Bateson “genetics” 1905; Johannsen “gene” 1909. Locus = gene position. Genome = all chromosomes. Gene number varies (*Arabidopsis* ~25,500; *E. coli* 5,416; *Mycoplasma genitalium* 517). Human cell 23 pairs. Genetic code: 3-base codon → amino acid (Nirenberg 1961).
- Transcription: DNA → mRNA. Translation: mRNA → protein at ribosomes. Each parent passes one gene copy.
- Functions: control DNA/RNA; make proteins/enzymes (e.g. haemoglobin). Phenotype = genotype + environment. Polygenes. Mutations → alleles; natural selection and drift.
- Chromosome = long DNA with many genes; histone packaging; visible in division; equal split of DNA. Errors: Down, Turner, leukaemia.
- Gene vs chromosome table: gene = DNA/RNA code on a chromosome, not visible; chromosome = pack of genes, DNA+RNA+histones, visible, large rearrangements.
- Relationship: genes sit on chromosomes; chromosomes transmit them in mitosis/meiosis; expression/regulation; linkage; abnormalities (deletions, duplications, inversions); evolution; CRISPR etc.
- Review: gene is basis of heredity (true); gene on chromosome; evolve by mutation/selection/drift; functions include protein coding; chromosome carries many genes; disorders Down/Turner; 23 pairs (46); histones; localization, transmission, expression.
